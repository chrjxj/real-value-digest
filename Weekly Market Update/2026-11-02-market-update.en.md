# Market Update – November 2, 2026

> Author: Sandy Leeds. Source: [original PDF](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5c42418c372b96e73b4fec1b/t/6ae7430a04c2972452d0ec11/1793540875025/2026+November+2.pdf). Extracted from all 6 pages. Page headers and footers omitted; wording and figures retained.


## #1. It was another mixed week for stocks: Russell 2000 +1.30%; S&P 500 -0.57%; DJIA -0.85%; and Nasdaq -2.00%. The equal-weighted S&P 500 rose 0.82%. For the month, S&P 500 Pure Value rose 6.20% and Pure Growth fell 9.11%.
- The 10-year yield dropped to 4.93% and the 2-year fell to 4.60%.
- WTI crude finished the week at $102.89.

The big stories of the week included:
- Anthropic filed its S-1
    - The possibility that Anthropic could be on a path to profitability is crucial because of its central position as a demand driver for the entire AI infrastructure buildout
- Several large tech companies released their earnings and scared investors
- Two big concerns are getting investors’ attention:
    - The concentration and quality of earnings
    - AI’s need for capital
        - OpenAI raised less than half of the $30B it had targeted in its latest funding round. It cut its target amount from $30B to $20B. The deal values the company at $1.25T – less than the $1.4T it was hoping for. (WSJ)
            - Anthropic more than doubled its revenue to $11.6B from Q1 to Q2 while OpenAI grew sales by 18% to $6.7B. (Bloomberg)
        - S&P Global Ratings lowered the outlook on Meta’s AA- credit rating to negative due to elevated AI spending, rising leverage, and limited ability to monetize its AI investments. (Bloomberg)
- The FOMC met and left rates unchanged


## #2. The cracks in the AI trade grew deeper this week.
- The week started with excitement about Anthropic’s S-1, but tech earnings reversed that
    - There are fears about slowing growth, dwindling cash, and cannibalized business lines.
- Nasdaq had its worst month in more than a year. More than $2T in market value has been wiped out from the Nasdaq 100 this month
    - Big Tech earnings intensified investor scrutiny on the ROI of AI capex
    - Meta, Alphabet, and Amazon raised spending forecasts
        - Spending more plus higher interest rates
- Midterm elections may decide the extent to which the data-center backlash leads to restrictions on development
- The Magnificent Seven are now expected to report slower Q3 earnings growth than the remaining members of the S&P 500 (WSJ) (Reuters)


## #3. Anthropic released its S-1 for its IPO.
- Anthropic will be the public market’s first pure-play developer of advanced AI models. The ticker will be ANT and it’s looking at a valuation of ~$1.5T. It hopes to sell ~$100B of shares.
    - It was valued at $965B in a funding round in May
- Half-year revenue grew 1,368% YoY to $16.3B (from $1.2B in H1 2025)
    - But revenue only grew 45% QoQ in Q3 (from $11.6B to $16.8B)
    - Anthropic said more than half of Fortune 500 companies and over 30% of Global 2000 firms were customers, each generating at least $100K in revenue for Anthropic during the 12 months ended September 30
    - It acknowledged that open-weight models are a threat to pricing power
- Anthropic spent $8.9B on computing power and infrastructure in the six months ended June 30, a 283% increase YoY (up from $2.3B)
    - ANT’s spending on computing power fell from 251% of sales in 2025 Q1 to 48% of sales in 2026 Q2.
        - The company claims that improvements in its AI models have made it faster and more efficient to develop and serve its technology.
- Net loss for the first half of the year was $80.7B – largely due to convertible bonds
    - Operating loss was $693MM
    - Adjusted operating income was positive $1.62B – this excludes $2B of stock-based compensation, noncash charitable contributions, and a $1.5B litigation charge
        - Versus a loss of $1.85B a year ago
    - The company reported positive operating cash flow of $3.9B in the first half
- It has committed ~$630B to compute deals (data-center capacity and chip leases). It’s possible that there are $145B to $170B in additional commitments.
    - This is a lot for a company with $16B of sales in the first half of 2026
- There were 94 pages of risk factors, ranging from potential misuse and security threats to concerns that advanced systems could produce outputs that “could be interpreted as coercive, deceptive, or manipulative,” including behavior resembling blackmail
- Anthropic appears to have burned through only a tiny part of the $141B it has raised since inception. It has $130B on its balance sheet.
- The downside:
    - Growth is slowing
    - The company is paying higher prices to access scarce computing power.
        - Almost half of Anthropic’s revenue in the first six months of this year came from sales made through the cloud businesses of GOOGL, AMZN, and MSFT. Anthropic’s spending on sales, marketing and distribution partnerships such as these chewed up 19% of revenue in the first half of 2026, up from 10% in the same period a year before.
    - A shift towards directly purchasing AI chips will use up significant cash flow
        - Anthropic is struggling to secure the computing power it needs. This is why it is starting to develop more of their own computing infrastructure, rather than relying solely on cloud providers.
            - The lab will become more capital-intensive (Reuters) (Barron’s) (MarketWatch) (Bloomberg) (Bloomberg) (Bloomberg) (WSJ) (WSJ) (Economist) (WSJ)


## #4. Earnings news mostly scared investors:
- Alphabet’s stock dropped 13% on Thursday (down 12.6% for week)
    - Reported 25% revenue growth for quarter, including 103% for cloud division (with a $561B backlog – and more than 55% of it should be recognized as revenue within next 24 months)
        - But revenue from search advertising has decelerated
            - Is AI cannibalizing the business?
    - Search queries were growing at the fastest rate in five years as users embraced the new “AI mode” search
        - But queries in that mode were monetized at a lower rate than in traditional search
        - Instead of revenue for each click on a link, AI is giving a single answer
    - FCF was negative $9.1B
        - Spent $51.4B in capex in Q3 (up from $44.9B in Q2)
    - Google has future commitments to buy resources such as computing power and energy worth $924B (WSJ) (Reuters) (Bloomberg) (WSJ) (Economist)
- Amazon – reported FCF of -$42.8B – driven by capex of $77.26B ($20B above estimates). Also had slow growth in its retail and advertising business. Stock was down 3.08% for week.
    - A positive was that Amazon Web Services grew 39% YoY. (WSJ) (Reuters)
- Meta – plans for a record $97.5B capital raise heightened concerns about shareholder dilution and whether other tech companies will raise capital. Down 4.30% for week.
- Microsoft – planning to raise ~$25B through its first U.S. investment-grade bond sale in more than nine years. Stock was down 2.00% for week.
    - Cloud business (Azure) grew 48% (WSJ)
- Intel – revenue forecasts missed analysts’ estimates. The stock dropped 29% on Friday (down 24.38% for week)
    - Forecast a prolonged slowdown in personal-computer sales
        - Makers of laptops and desktops are struggling with shortages of memory semiconductors and surging component prices
    - Supply-chain problems with data center components (Bloomberg)
- The stock selloffs expose the tensions escalating over big tech’s transition to new AI- powered products, and the spending required to get there.
    - The companies have been issuing unprecedented levels of debt, selling equity, signing multibillion-dollar data center deals and taking on trillions of dollars in future obligations to pay for a worldwide build-out of AI infrastructure.
        - All of this is happening while new products are eroding the profitability of some of their legacy offerings and drawing attention away from them.
    - But remember that cloud computing was good for AMZN, MSFT, and GOOGL. (Bloomberg)


## #5. Strong earnings projections have allowed investors to not worry about higher rates or the Middle East. But there’s also a lot to think about with earnings.
- S&P 500 Q3 earnings growth is expected to be 30%. But just two companies (NVDA and MU) account for 34% of this. Ten companies (including AVGO and META) account for two-thirds of the growth. Some of the others are oil and gas giants.
    - The risk is that AI doesn’t turn out to be profitable.
    - On the positive side, even without the top 10 stocks, the rest of the S&P 500 is growing 10%. In addition, the lower P/E reflects the possibility that investors are already factoring in that the supersize earnings won’t last forever. (WSJ)
        - Of course, if you look at the trailing P/E, it’s ~26, which is 14% above its 10-year average.
- The IT sector is expected to grow earnings 67%. Its earnings also impact utilities and industrials.
    - But the narrow breadth also raises the odds of turbulence. Much of the economy and market now rest on the ability of a few hyperscalers to prove that AI is profitable and worth further investments in more chips and infrastructure.
    - When hyperscalers purchase chips, this results in immediate recognition of revenue and earnings for the seller. The chips aren’t expensed until they are put into use. This has a positive impact on S&P 500 earnings.
- The war has increased earnings for some companies.
    - Energy industry net income is expected to increase by 135% in Q3 YoY; aerospace and defense is expected to see 50% growth; materials (war-impacted petrochemicals, fertilizers and metals) are expected to grow 39%
    - For companies not linked to the war or AI, 4.5% growth is expected (barely beating 3.7% inflation)
- Analysts don’t do well making predictions at the end of a bull market.
    - Since 1992, the average annual S&P 500 EPS growth was 9.5%, but the average growth predicted by analysts was 22.3% (more than double).
        - But when realized EPS declined, the rolling 12-month average growth rate was -4.5% while projected growth was 18.8%.
- Research shows that companies in the decile with the highest long-term EPS forecasts usually underperform the rest of the market by a wide margin over the following year. The long-term EPS growth forecast for the S&P 500 is currently 16.7% (in the top 10% for this century).
    - NVDA, AMD, INTC, AVGO, PLTR, and MU have particularly high long-term EPS expectations.
- Energy shocks usually hit the economy with a lag
    - A Dallas Fed paper found that industrial production typically declines with a delay of about 10 months
    - The S&P 500 consumer discretionary sector is expected to see just 1.8% growth this quarter; consumer staples 1.3% (Bloomberg) (Reuters)


## #6. The earnings inconsistency…
- The large loss from the convertible notes being converted into equity resulted in large gains for Anthropic’s investors, including AMZN and GOOGL.
    - NVDA does not include gains or losses
- If it’s true that the loss should be excluded from ANT, then the gains shouldn’t be included with AMZN and GOOGL.
    - AMZN booked ~$63B of gains on equity investments in private companies, primarily Anthropic (most of AMZN’s pretax profit)
    - GOOGL has recorded ~$136B in net gains on equity investments during the first half of the year (most of its pretax profit)
        - Don’t know how much is tied to Anthropic
    - During Q2, gains at these two companies represented ~15% of S&P 500 earnings (WSJ)


## #7. The Fed
- The Fed held rates steady at 3.75% - 4.00% with a 10-2 vote. Fed governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissented in favor of a quarter-point increase.
- The statement hinted at another rate increase, saying that more information will help the committee determine the appropriate policy stance to ensure a timely return to 2% inflation.
- Warsh said the economy was strong (an upgrade from solid) and inflation was still too high, yet the FOMC didn’t raise rates. He didn’t give an answer as to why rates weren’t raised, other than to say that the committee was “interrogating reality.”
    - Words can’t bring down inflation. And as Jamie McGeever (Reuters) pointed out, this is especially true when the words don’t say much at all.
- The Fed also announced that it would vote on interest rates at only seven of its eight policy meetings next year, using the other one to discuss monetary policy strategy.
- Issues that investors are thinking about:
    - Kevin Warsh has said that AI will be a disinflationary force, increasing productivity and bolstering American competitiveness.
        - How will Warsh respond if there is a funding crunch?
            - Maybe this is why the Fed didn’t raise rates?
    - There’s a common view that AI is propping up the economy and the AI sector is relatively interest-rate insensitive.
        - Warsh said that higher rates are working into the cracks of AI.
            - This made investors believe that the FOMC might not aggressively raise rates (resulting in a steeper curve)
    - Financial conditions seem to be changing, and these are a key variable that the FOMC considers in setting policy. Financial conditions include short- and long-term borrowing costs and stock prices.
        - While the Fed sets short-term rates, the U.S. economy runs on long rates and stock market valuation
        - You can ask how financial conditions are impacting businesses and consumers.
            - It doesn’t seem like financial conditions are helping to bring down price pressures.
        - Higher rates haven’t been an impediment for commercial and industrial loan growth, but they have been a restraint for residential real estate lending. (WSJ) (Reuters) (Bloomberg) (WSJ) (Reuters)


## #8. The AI race is turning into a financing arms race.
- Citadel’s co-CIO said that extraordinary capital demand underpinning the AI revolution is clashing with wary lenders unwilling to take on all that risk.
    - This will force a reconciliation between stock-pickers chasing multiples of their winnings and creditors who need to make sure they are repaid
        - High valuations were based on growth. Without capex, growth could be gone.
- Global credit markets have been flashing warning signs
    - The risk premium on junk-rated debt has surged
    - There have been comments that the debt market will destroy neoclouds
- Recent problems:
    - Meta said it would bet more aggressively on AI
        - Future spending commitments rose to $825B
        - Even announced a new class of stock – showing that it can’t just rely on borrowed money
    - Anthropic may spend $750B on additional computing power in coming years
- The credit market isn’t structured to raise hundreds of billions of dollars of story debt
    - With all of the credit that will be issued, investors can be choosy
        - Credit investors can barely predict three years out, let alone five years out (Bloomberg)


## #9. The AI debt machine is starting to choke on supply
- Across all types of U.S. corporate debt markets, AI-linked issuance totaled $394B in 2025 and $655B through mid-October of this year. Next year, it could approach $1T.
- Early signs suggest that the credit market is approaching the limits of how much more debt it can absorb
    - Yields are soaring into double digits on bonds and loans funding the AI infrastructure buildout
        - At least 18 data-center financings across the junk-bond and leveraged loan markets now carry double-digit yields
        - Bankers are finding it more difficult to offload risk to insurers and other investors
    - The cost of using credit default swaps to insure against losses on the debt of companies from AVGO to SPCX and ORCL has spiked
        - To levels more typical of junk-rated borrowers
    - Neocloud stocks dropped
- Citadel is cautioning clients that even the biggest names in tech could get ensnared as they put their balance sheets behind scores of projects critical to AI ambitions
    - There’s just a disconnect between the trillions of dollars needed in the future and what the markets can provide
- One reason that financing is becoming more difficult is the growing concern over cancellation rights and other contractual off-ramps that could leave lenders exposed if a major customer walks away
    - Last month, Oracle issued a force-majeure notice seeking to defer payments if a massive New Mexico center were delayed
    - Other risks include technological change, construction delays, permitting hurdles and access to power
- Credit investors are starting to recognize that much of the debt neoclouds still need to raise may ultimately depend on backing from tech giants already burdened with their own massive financing needs
    - Whether via explicit guarantees, long-term contracts or simply the expectation that they’ll step in if projects run into trouble, chipmakers and hyperscalers could find themselves shouldering an increasing share of the risk (Bloomberg) (WSJ)
