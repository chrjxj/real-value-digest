## #2. Currently, negative sentiment about AI is focused on three issues:


- Will AI capex generate sufficient returns to justify the massive capex?

- Can the AI-related firms raise enough capital for a $10T buildout over the next seven years?

- Will there be enough electricity to power the data centers?


## #3. The biggest move of the week was the tech drop on Thursday. Meta seemed to be at the center of
 the storm. Meta fell 7% on Thursday, SPCX fell 9%, and NVDA fell 4%. Lam Research fell 12%. SOXX fell 6.8% on Thursday and 3.1% for the week.

- Meta jumped 7% in after-hours trading on Wednesday after announcing earnings. But then fears about spending and the announcement of a new class of stock reversed those gains and pushed the stock down to a loss of 7% on Thursday.

    - The CDS premium for Meta rose to a record high.

- Meta’s FCF went negative for the first time since going public.

    - Agents (like Muse) use a tremendous amount of computing power.

            - Muse is also problematic because people don’t trust Meta (or Zuckerberg) on privacy issues.

    - They forecast $135B - $145B of capex in 2026.

- Investors are growing increasingly skeptical that there will be enough user demand to generate required returns (to justify the massive investment).

    - The percentage of companies reporting productivity gains from AI actually fell from 42% in Q1 to 37% in Q3. (KPMG)

- Meta announced plans to create a new class of nonvoting stock to raise capital for developing and serving AI models. The reversal from Wednesday afternoon to Thursday could be described as a shift from excitement over record Q3 revenue to anxiety over securing capital to fund the AI race.

    - Meta wants to beat competitors to available capital.

            - Meta disclosed that its future spending commitments had risen from nearly $700B in July to approximately $825B.

    - There is fear that rising borrowing costs will motivate tech companies to issue more shares. (WSJ) (WSJ) (Reuters) (Reuters) (Barron’s)


## #4. Thursday’s move was about more than just Meta. The Nasdaq dropped 2.60%.


- The S&P 500 dropped more than 1% on Thursday, its worst showing since July

    - Yet more than 300 stocks finished higher

    - The market is like a seesaw

            - AI on one side and everything else on the other

- Losses in popular AI stocks drove the market-cap-weighted index lower

    - SOXX fell by ~6.8% (and is down more than 27% since its June peak)

- This was the eighth straight week that the equal-weighted S&P 500 and the SOXX moved in different directions

    - Longest such streak on record (data back to the mid-1990s).

    - A sign of market rotation (rather than widespread selling)

- Others are less optimistic. The S&P 500 has moved in the opposite direction from most of its members during more than 30% of all trading days in 2026, by far the most in any calendar year in the past 30 years.

    - At some point, you could see a higher-correlation selloff (MarketWatch)


## #5. Oil flows from the Persian Gulf have been increasing, yet the price of oil has also been increasing.
 Possible reasons for higher oil prices:

- The Houthis conducted drone strikes Wednesday on Saudi airports and the Saudi Aramco Shaybah oil field

- Trump has warned that the U.S. could attack after the midterm elections (Nov. 3)

- Tehran said that it would respond with strikes on pipelines and airports in oil-rich Persian Gulf countries

    - There are also reports that Iran will destroy underwater fiber and attack U.S. citizens

- The markets aren’t just pricing availability; they’re also pricing reliability. There are concerns about energy infrastructure and routes. (MarketWatch) (Reuters)

    - Evidence of risk: Benchmark rates for a tanker carrying 2MM barrels of crude from the Middle East to China are ~$1.4MM per day, more than 12X pre-war levels.

            - Tanker insurance costs have risen to ~10% of the cargo value, up from ~3%.

- The 10-year UST yield fell 19.3 bps to 4.952% on Thursday, marking its largest daily yield drop since March 10, 2023. Only 15 days ago, the yield was 40 bps higher (intraday). Possible reasons for lower yields:

    - Tech stocks plunged (flight to safety)

            - Increasing doubts about the AI front

    - Escalation in the Middle East

    - Ongoing and accelerating problems in France

    - Questions about whether the Fed will be able to continue to increase rates (especially if the AI trade falters)

            - The 2-year UST yield dropped 18.8 bps

    - Short-covering in the UST market

            - There had been a lot of negative sentiment

- There have only been 15 trading days in the past decade in which the 10-year UST yield has moved by 19 bps in either direction

- It’s interesting to see that yields dropped despite higher oil prices (MarketWatch) (Barron’s) (MarketWatch)


## #7. Thinking About AI


- AI’s implications for the broader market are enormous.

    - According to JPM, a group of 41 AI-related stocks has accounted for 75% of S&P 500 returns, 80% of earnings growth, and 90% of capital spending growth since November 2022 (ChatGPT’s launch).

    - Bond investors seemed to get scared first. Bond investors are paid to be bearish and stock investors are paid to be bullish.

        - We’re seeing bonds trade at low levels (CDS at high levels) while AI stocks had been doing very well (but have struggled recently)

            - The S&P 500 and Nasdaq hit record highs just two weeks ago.

            - CoreWeave’s October 2031 debt has dropped 20% since its April issuance.

- FOMO has driven data-center developers to build as much computing capacity as possible (as quickly as possible). All of the players in this infrastructure boom, from upstarts such as Nscale to giants like Alphabet, are focused on being first to market and locking in contracts.

    - The five large hyperscalers had started 2026 with plans to do $630B of capex on data centers in 2027. That number is now $1.1T.

    - But investors funding the projects are getting nervous

        - Will spending on AI services be enough to justify the investments?

        - Political opposition is growing. Construction delays, equipment failures and power constraints are hurting developers and slowing projects.

- Can these projects generate enough returns?

    - McKinsey and S&P Global estimate that the top six U.S. hyperscalers will spend more than $7T on data centers between 2025 and 2030.

    - A Columbia University researcher says that the cost of financing the AI buildout from last year to 2032 will be $10.3T.

        - For a 10% return, he estimates that annual revenue will need to be ~$3.55T by 2032. This is over 10% of expected 2032 GDP.

    - Bain said that the likely revenue from corporate and consumer spending by 2031 would be less than $2T

        - Far below the $6T needed to justify planned investment

    - Gartner estimated that data center owners would face a revenue shortfall of $6T by 2032 compared with infrastructure spending

        - This will result in massive AI capital asset write-downs

    - A GS analyst said that the five hyperscalers plus SPCX need to generate an extra $300B annually in AI services revenue in 2026 and 2027 just to break even on the capital spending

        - They need to deliver more than $400B to make a 10% return

        - As of Q2, the extra cloud-services income they were getting above pre-AI trends was just $70B annualized.

- Token usage is still rising steadily, but spending appears to have topped out (i.e., token prices have fallen). This implies that revenue at OpenAI and Anthropic may not continue to rise as spectacularly as hoped.

    - AI stocks are sensitive to expectations about AI usage and revenue. This could be an issue.

- The silver lining for the cloud providers is the backlog of revenue they should get from contracts due to start once the new data centers come online

    - The Remaining Performance Obligations reported by GOOGL, AMZN, and MSFT totaled $1.7T at the end of June

        - This is up from $700B a year ago

    - But there are several reasons to be cautious about RPOs:

        - They aren’t guaranteed and some can be renegotiated

        - Many aren’t due to start for several years, and they can be highly dependent on a single client

            - Half of MSFT’s $684B backlog is OpenAI

                - OpenAI’s recent guidance was disappointing

            - One-fifth of AMZN’s $496B is Anthropic

                - Anthropic’s growth slowed from 140% to 45%

                - Meta and Microsoft have both told their own staff to cut back on their use of Claude

- Energy supply-chain chokepoints, including insufficient electricity and inadequate grid connections, mean many AI chips are sitting and depreciating in warehouses.

    - Morgan Stanley says that U.S. data center developers will face a 30% to 40% power shortfall through 2028, equivalent to 34% of total chip demand.

    - This week, Microsoft pulled out of a deal to anchor a flagship data center in Britain with Nvidia-backed Nscale because power is not expected until the 2030s.

        - This has cast doubt on Nscale’s planned U.S. stock listing. (Bloomberg) (Reuters)


## #8. The AI Boom is Setting Up the Fed for a Rate Seesaw (by Bill Dudley)


- The wild card for U.S. interest rates is the trajectory of the AI investment boom and its impact on stock market valuation.

- The boom is pushing rates higher in two ways:

    - Investment spending is boosting growth and straining financing capacity

    - The boom has lifted the stock market – lifting household wealth and spending

- We don’t know the magnitude and duration of the boom. But:

    - The direct impact on U.S. economic growth is likely to diminish next year because the increase in investment spending in 2027 is likely to be smaller than the increase in 2026

        - Constraints in building new data centers, tougher financing conditions, political opposition

    - The impact on real interest rates caused by the pressure of AI investment on the nation’s savings pool will continue to increase

        - This will be the bigger impact, and it will reduce consumer spending on durable goods and housing, as well as non-AI capex

- In the longer term, the upward pressure on rates will reverse as the AI investment boom peaks and then declines (the buildout will be done or financing won’t be sufficient)

    - AI investment will then be a drag on growth and will absorb a smaller share of savings

    - The equity market will likely retrench as the boom-generated profits subside

    - These two factors will slow the economy, and the Fed will cut rates

- The problem is that the timing and intensity of the reversal are highly uncertain

    - Dudley thinks it will be sooner than anticipated because the AI providers won’t be able to generate sufficient revenue to achieve an acceptable ROI

        - Before we get to this point, AI providers without strong cash flows will lose access to the capital markets

- Dudley thinks the Fed should act more quickly for two reasons:

    - The AI boom is pushing the economy along fast enough to keep the labor market at full employment

    - This is happening when inflation is still elevated

        - And the risk of a policy mistake is tilted toward the inflation side (expectations could become unanchored – damaging Fed credibility)

- The market is pricing in a 15% chance of a rate hike next week

    - Warsh isn’t going to surprise the market

    - The Fed vice-chair (Phillip Jefferson) and New York Fed President John Williams tempered market expectations – likely with the blessing of Warsh (Bloomberg)
